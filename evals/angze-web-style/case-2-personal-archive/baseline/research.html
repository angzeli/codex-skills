The Slow Atlas

A longitudinal record of one hundred overlooked urban sites: service alleys, retaining walls, drainage channels, vacant thresholds, and the edges of transit land.

Each site is revisited twice a year using the same compact protocol. The project follows heat, moisture, spontaneous plants, ambient sound, and visible repair without ranking places as successful or failed.

Cooperative · Open methods

Small-Data Commons

A framework for publishing modest environmental observations without overstating their reach. It favors legible provenance, explicit gaps, and formats that remain readable without specialized software.

The commons is being tested by six neighborhood groups documenting shade, street-tree stress, and summer surface temperatures. Participants retain ownership of their records and decide which interpretations become public.

Useful Instruments

A practical record of keeping field tools in service: replacing sensor cables, checking drift, documenting firmware, and designing enclosures that can be opened instead of discarded.

The project asks a technological question with an ecological consequence: what observations become possible when equipment is built around repair rather than replacement?
