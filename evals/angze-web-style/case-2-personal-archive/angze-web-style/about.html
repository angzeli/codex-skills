Biography & colophon

I study how small environments remember change.

Mira Vale is a fictional urban ecologist and essayist based near the edge of a large harbor. Her research follows ordinary habitats—the moss on a wall, shade beneath a bridge, the warm air behind a kitchen—and the infrastructure that shapes them.

She trained in environmental science and later worked in civic mapping, where she became interested in the distance between measured places and lived ones. Her current practice combines repeat field observation, small repairable sensors, walking, and long-form nonfiction.

Most projects in this archive unfold over years. That pace is intentional. Seasonal return makes slow change visible and allows an initial interpretation to be corrected.

A note on practice

This website is a public notebook, not a complete record. Published essays have dates and stable titles. Field fragments may be revised, and their revision dates are noted. Personal encounters are anonymized unless a person has explicitly agreed to be named.

Measurements are shared with methods and limitations. Anecdotes are never presented as datasets; datasets are never asked to stand in for the places and people from which they came.

Elsewhere, slowly

I send a short seasonal letter four times a year. There is no automated signup in this static archive; write to hello@miravale.example if you would like to receive it. The address is fictional, like its author.
